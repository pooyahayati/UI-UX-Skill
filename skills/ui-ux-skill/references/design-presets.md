# Design Presets and Variation System

Presets are vocabulary and starting points, not templates.

Never make two unrelated products look the same merely because they selected the same preset.

Combine product context, users, frequency, density, brand, typography, palette, surface, direction, theme, and device priority.

## Preset matrix

Use this as a directional heuristic, not a rigid specification.

| Dimension | Minimal | Professional | Executive | Data-Dense | Modern SaaS | Premium | Technical |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Density | Low–Med | Balanced | Medium | High | Balanced | Low–Med | High |
| Typography | Quiet | Clear | Strong summary hierarchy | Compact | Contemporary | Refined | Precise |
| Surface | Sparse | Restrained | Grouped summaries | Flat/bordered | Flexible | Refined layers | Flat/bordered |
| Radius | Low–Med | Medium | Medium | Low | Medium | Medium–High | Low |
| Elevation | Minimal | Low | Low–Med | Minimal | Low–Med | Controlled | Minimal |
| Navigation | Simple | Stable | Summary-oriented | Compact | Flexible | Calm | Compact |
| Tables | Secondary | Strong | Supporting | Primary | Strong | Secondary | Primary |
| Charts | Selective | Purposeful | Decision summary | Analytical | Purposeful | Curated | Diagnostic |
| Motion | Very low | Low | Low | Very low | Low–Med | Subtle | Very low |
| Card use | Minimal | Moderate | Summary groups | Low | Moderate | Selective | Low |

Adapt each dimension to actual task frequency and user expertise.

## Minimal

Use restraint, strong typography, few surfaces, low decoration, and clear whitespace.

Prefer:
- fewer containers
- strong grouping through spacing
- limited accent use
- low motion

Avoid becoming sparse at the expense of operational efficiency.

## Professional

Use balanced density, restrained brand expression, clear hierarchy, stable patterns, and moderate surfaces.

Prefer:
- predictable navigation
- clear action hierarchy
- strong table/form usability
- neutral semantic surfaces

## Executive

Emphasize summary, change, exceptions, decisions, and high-level comparison.

Prefer:
- compact decision summaries
- meaningful deltas
- exception-first storytelling
- clear time/comparison context

Do not hide important caveats behind visual polish.

## Data-Dense

Optimize scanability, compact controls, table quality, shortcuts, and information throughput.

Prefer:
- strong column alignment
- compact but readable typography
- reduced decorative containers
- quick filters/saved views
- high information-to-chrome ratio

Do not confuse density with clutter.

## Modern SaaS

Use contemporary but restrained product patterns, compact hierarchy, and flexible responsive behavior.

Prefer:
- clear component hierarchy
- balanced whitespace
- pragmatic responsive adaptation

Avoid generic template aesthetics, gratuitous gradients, and oversized headings.

## Premium

Use refined typography, spacing, surface treatment, and brand expression.

Prefer:
- fewer, higher-quality visual gestures
- careful typography
- controlled elevation
- restrained motion

Avoid decorative luxury that slows work.

## Technical

Favor precision, system status, explicit state, compact data, and low ambiguity.

Prefer:
- compact information
- system/state clarity
- diagnostic details
- predictable controls
- low decoration

## Variation dimensions

A preset must influence more than color:

- typography
- spacing
- density
- navigation
- border and surface treatment
- radius and elevation
- icon behavior
- chart treatment
- motion
- card usage
- table emphasis
- action hierarchy

Reference screenshots, supplied brand systems, and domain requirements may override preset assumptions when they better express the user's intended direction.

## Avoid preset lock-in

After selecting a preset:

- validate against product tasks
- adapt density to frequency
- adapt charts to actual questions
- adapt navigation to destination count
- adapt typography to language/script
- adapt motion to operational risk
- adapt branding to supplied identity

The preset is a starting direction, never the final design by itself.
