# Executive Dashboard Rules

Use this local rule pack when the active product route includes `dashboard` and the dashboard is primarily for executives, senior managers, business owners, or decision-makers who need a high-level view of organizational performance.

This is an internal Dashboard mode, not a separate Product Type.

## Primary job

An executive dashboard should help the user answer:

- Are we on track?
- What changed materially?
- Where are we off target?
- What requires executive attention?
- What decision or escalation is needed?
- What can remain delegated?

Do not turn an executive dashboard into an operational control room.

## Executive summary layer

Prioritize a concise summary of:

- strategic outcomes;
- target vs actual;
- material variance;
- trend direction;
- major risks/opportunities;
- exceptions requiring attention.

A dashboard may contain only a few prominent metrics if those are the metrics that matter.

Do not fill space with KPIs merely because executive dashboards are expected to have cards.

## Targets and baselines

For strategic metrics, show relevant context such as:

- target/plan;
- prior period;
- forecast;
- benchmark;
- budget;
- tolerance/range.

Avoid isolated numbers without a basis for interpretation.

If a target changes, make the effective period understandable.

## Trend over snapshot

Prefer trend/context over a single large number when direction matters.

A current value can be misleading without:

- prior period;
- target;
- volatility;
- seasonality;
- forecast;
- event annotation.

Do not imply that a short-term fluctuation is strategically meaningful without evidence.

## Exceptions and materiality

Executive attention is scarce.

Emphasize exceptions based on:

- business consequence;
- magnitude;
- persistence;
- strategic relevance;
- uncertainty.

Do not escalate ordinary operational noise into the executive layer.

Where useful, define thresholds or materiality rules.

## Forecast and uncertainty

When forecasts are shown:

- label them as forecast/projection;
- distinguish actual from projected;
- show confidence/range when meaningful;
- make assumptions discoverable;
- avoid visual treatment that makes uncertain values appear equally certain as observed values.

Do not present a model output as a guaranteed future outcome.

## Portfolio and segment comparison

When comparing business units, products, regions, teams, or channels:

- keep metric definitions consistent;
- show size/context so small segments are not misread;
- support sorting by consequence;
- identify outliers;
- enable drill-down without destroying overview context.

Avoid ranking entities on a metric that is not comparable.

## Decision and follow-up

If the dashboard reveals an issue, provide a path to:

- drill into the relevant report/dashboard;
- open the responsible business area;
- inspect supporting records;
- view owner/status;
- record or navigate to an action when product scope supports it.

The executive dashboard does not need to perform every operational action itself.

## Narrative and annotations

For major events, consider concise annotations for:

- launch;
- outage;
- policy change;
- campaign;
- acquisition;
- pricing change;
- one-off accounting/event effects.

Annotations should explain meaningful context, not become a running commentary.

## Time horizon

Choose default time ranges according to the decision cadence.

Examples may include:

- weekly operating review;
- monthly performance;
- quarterly planning;
- year-to-date progress.

Do not default every executive view to "last 7 days."

## Presentation density

Favor scanability and comparison over dense interaction.

Avoid:

- high-frequency refresh;
- large operational tables;
- excessive filters;
- complex multi-step interactions;
- alert streams;
- detailed logs.

A compact exception table may still be appropriate when it supports executive decisions.

## Responsive/presentation mode

Executive dashboards may be viewed on:

- laptop;
- tablet;
- meeting-room display;
- exported report.

Preserve the core story when interaction is limited.

For presentation/wall display, do not rely on hover-only explanation.

## Validation checklist

Validate:

- strategic question clarity;
- target/baseline context;
- material exception visibility;
- actual vs forecast distinction;
- segment comparability;
- drill-down path;
- representative executive time horizon;
- meeting/presentation display when relevant;
- accessibility and non-color status meaning;
- RTL/LTR reading order and analytical meaning.
