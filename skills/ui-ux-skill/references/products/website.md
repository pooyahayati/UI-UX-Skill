# Website Product Pack

Use only when the active product route includes `website`.

This Product Pack applies to public-facing websites whose primary purpose is communication, discovery, trust, content consumption, lead generation, or service/product presentation rather than performing authenticated application work.

Typical examples include:

- company and corporate websites
- service-business websites
- personal-brand and portfolio websites
- blogs and publications
- campaign and landing pages
- public informational sites
- SEO-driven content sites

## Primary objective

A website should help visitors quickly understand:

- who the organization/person is;
- what is offered;
- why it is relevant;
- why it should be trusted;
- what the visitor should do next.

Do not design from decoration first.

Establish the visitor intent, content hierarchy, trust signals, and conversion path before selecting visual treatments.

## Website vs web application

Use `website` when the primary experience is public-facing communication, discovery, content, or conversion.

Use `web-application` when the primary experience is an authenticated or stateful application used to perform ongoing work.

A product may contain both:

- public marketing/content site -> `website`
- signed-in product area -> `web-application`

Route each affected surface independently.

Do not apply app-shell conventions to a public marketing site without a clear reason.

## Information architecture

Before broad visual work, identify:

- primary visitor segments;
- top visitor questions;
- primary and secondary conversion goals;
- main navigation destinations;
- high-value content paths;
- legal/trust/support surfaces;
- search needs where relevant.

Navigation should reflect visitor intent rather than internal company structure.

Avoid deep, arbitrary menu nesting.

## Homepage and landing pages

The first screen should make the page purpose understandable without requiring interpretation.

Where relevant, establish:

- clear value proposition;
- supporting proof or differentiation;
- primary action;
- secondary action;
- credibility/trust evidence;
- relevant next-step content.

Avoid generic hero sections that consume large vertical space without conveying useful information.

Do not use decorative metrics, testimonials, logos, or badges unless they support a real trust decision.

## Content hierarchy

Use typography, spacing, grouping, media, and semantic sections to support scanning.

Prioritize:

- clarity before novelty;
- meaningful headings;
- concise supporting text;
- useful visual evidence;
- accessible calls to action;
- content order that reflects visitor questions.

Do not turn every content block into a card.

## Conversion and forms

Treat conversion as a user task, not merely a button color.

For forms:

- ask only for information needed at that stage;
- use explicit labels;
- explain errors near the relevant fields;
- preserve valid values after validation errors;
- make success and next steps clear;
- distinguish high-commitment from low-commitment actions.

Do not use manipulative urgency, misleading controls, or hidden opt-ins.

## Trust and credibility

Where relevant, make trust evidence understandable and verifiable.

Examples:

- real company/person identity;
- service scope;
- qualifications or credentials;
- client/customer evidence;
- case studies;
- policies;
- contact information;
- location/service area;
- pricing or pricing context;
- security/privacy signals when material.

Do not fabricate social proof, customer counts, awards, ratings, testimonials, or certifications.

## SEO and semantic structure

For SEO-driven websites, UI/UX should support semantic content structure rather than fight it.

Preserve or improve:

- meaningful page titles and heading hierarchy;
- crawlable primary content;
- descriptive link text;
- semantic landmarks;
- internal navigation;
- content discoverability;
- readable URL/deep-link behavior when within scope.

Do not hide essential content behind interaction patterns that materially reduce discoverability without a product reason.

SEO strategy remains outside pure visual design when it requires content or technical decisions owned by another specialist/head.

## Responsive behavior

Design for content priority, not simple desktop shrinkage.

Resolve:

- navigation transformation;
- readable line length;
- media scaling/cropping;
- CTA placement;
- multi-column collapse;
- tables or comparison content;
- sticky elements;
- forms;
- footer density;
- touch targets.

Do not preserve desktop spacing ratios mechanically on narrow screens.

## Performance and media

Public websites are often performance-sensitive because users may arrive from search, ads, social, or slow networks.

Prefer:

- appropriately sized responsive images;
- limited nonessential JavaScript;
- controlled font loading;
- predictable layout;
- progressive enhancement where appropriate;
- lazy loading that does not harm primary content usability.

When performance is in scope, read `../performance.md`.

## Accessibility

Accessibility applies to the whole visitor journey.

Validate:

- semantic structure;
- keyboard navigation;
- focus visibility;
- contrast;
- text scaling;
- form labels/errors;
- image alternatives;
- link purpose;
- motion/reduced-motion behavior;
- menus and dialogs;
- skip/navigation landmarks where appropriate.

## Brand and visual identity

A public website may carry more expressive brand presentation than an operational application, but branding must still support clarity and usability.

Use visual style to reinforce the product's real positioning.

Do not redesign the logo or identity unless that scope was approved.

## Persian and multilingual websites

For Persian-facing content, the required `persian-writing` specialist owns Persian-language validation.

This Product Pack owns website information architecture, page behavior, public-facing UX, responsive behavior, and website-specific presentation—not Persian linguistic rules.

For bilingual or multilingual sites:

- keep language switching discoverable;
- preserve language-specific direction;
- avoid broken mixed-direction content;
- maintain equivalent core navigation and conversion paths where appropriate.

## Validation

For website work, validate representative:

- homepage or primary landing page;
- at least one content/service page;
- primary navigation;
- primary conversion flow/form;
- trust/contact/legal surface where relevant;
- mobile and desktop layouts;
- active language/direction variants;
- error/validation/success states for forms;
- accessibility basics;
- rendered output;
- performance-sensitive media/layout behavior when in scope.

For SEO/content-heavy work, also verify semantic heading/navigation structure and content discoverability.

Do not claim conversion improvement, SEO uplift, or accessibility compliance without evidence.
