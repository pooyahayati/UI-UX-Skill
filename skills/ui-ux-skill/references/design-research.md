# Selected design research — optional, style-independent

Use only when a specific design question needs evidence beyond the inspected
product/approved foundation. This index is usable without Material selection;
reading an editorial article does not select a style, approve a design, add a
specialist or authorize an implementation library. Do not load every entry or
archive the site. A narrow fix with sufficient local evidence needs no research
tour. Preserve [discovery](discovery-and-profile.md) and
[handbook provenance](design-handbook.md).

## Distinguish evidence before using it

| Source class | What it can support | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official Material specification | Meaning of a selected system's roles/patterns | Suitability for every audience, owner consent, or availability in the project's library |
| Google Design editorial/research example | A contextual design hypothesis, practical question or study finding | Universal standard, our product's measured improvement, font script support or production compliance |
| Implementation documentation/repository | Actual supported API/assets/coverage for an inspected version | The whole design specification, mobile-web usability or authorization to install/migrate |

Open only the entry that addresses the active question. Read its relevant
context and limitations before adoption. Confirm volatile support/library facts
from the implementation source at the authorized decision, not from an article.
If a source is unavailable, label it unreviewed and use adequate product evidence
or ask about the material gap; do not fabricate research or approval.

## Emphasis and expression

- **Question:** Would stronger hierarchy or expressive treatment help the actual
  audience find key actions without losing familiarity?
- **Source:** [Expressive Design: Google's UX Research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research).
- **Practical inference:** Consider selective emphasis, then validate the product's
  workflow; preserve labels and established interaction patterns. Recommend a
  calmer treatment when the task/context calls for it, not by age stereotype.
- **Context/limits:** Google reports research on its tested designs. It also
  describes usability failures from unfamiliar patterns/removing labels and
  cautions that context matters. No percentage or speed result is a promised
  outcome for our product; expressive appearance is not mandatory Material use.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-06; official article text inspected. This practical
  recommendation is our inference, not a copied component rule.

## Font suitability

- **Question:** Does an unresolved font choice fit the content, role, audience and
  actual supported scripts/weights?
- **Source:** [Choosing Web Fonts: A Beginner's Guide](https://design.google/library/choosing-web-fonts-beginners-guide).
- **Practical inference:** Evaluate role, amount of text, glyph coverage, weight
  availability and representative mixed-script content before recommending a
  change. Start from approved assets; an already settled family needs no new vote.
- **Context/limits:** General editorial guidance and font examples, not a proof
  of licensing, current file coverage, browser loading or Persian shaping.
  Specific font recommendations are not universal defaults; inspect actual
  assets and render where relevant before claiming fit.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-06; official article text inspected. Keep the existing
  typography/asset-governance contract authoritative.

## Audience assumptions and use context

- **Question:** Are assumed reading ability, input needs, scripts or viewing
  conditions excluding real users of this product?
- **Source:** [Designing for Global Accessibility: Part 1](https://design.google/library/designing-global-accessibility-part-1).
- **Practical inference:** Check the product's real audience/context, use clear
  language and complementary cues, and plan representative accessibility and
  actual language-flow validation. Do not infer needs from a generic persona.
- **Context/limits:** Inclusive-design perspective with examples, not evidence
  that our users were studied or that every suggested interaction belongs in
  this product. It does not authorize new languages, voice input or extra roles.
  Current applicable standards and real-user evidence still govern acceptance.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-06; official indexed article text inspected. Adopt only
  relevant recommendations and record our inference separately.

## Using a finding in the product

Record the active question, source URL/review date, relevant finding versus our
inference, product/audience rationale, limitation, proposed or accepted adoption,
scope/revision and actual owner decision in the existing handbook. Link any
implemented value to its actual authority rather than copying a live setting.
Do not turn an article or a pleasing reference into user research, a rendered
check or acceptance. When later feedback contradicts the hypothesis, revisit
the affected decision rather than override the evidence.

This is a deliberately small starting index, not a complete Google Design
catalog, mandatory reading list or comprehensive literature review. Add a source
only when a real recurring decision justifies maintaining it. Independent/product
evaluation and current library assessment remain separate scoped work.
